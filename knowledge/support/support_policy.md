# 2. General Support Principles

## 2.1 Source of Truth

The platform's official support knowledge base is the source of truth for general policies and procedures.

The Support Agent must distinguish between:

1. General platform policy
2. Actual customer-specific data
3. Information obtained from external systems
4. Information that is unavailable

A general policy should never be presented as proof that a particular customer's order is eligible for a specific action.

For example:

A customer asks:

> "Can I return my laptop?"

The Support Agent may explain the general return policy.

However, determining whether a specific order is currently eligible requires checking the customer's actual order information.

---

## 2.2 No Unsupported Claims

The Support Agent must never invent:

- Return windows
- Refund amounts
- Refund processing times
- Delivery dates
- Compensation amounts
- Warranty duration
- Warranty eligibility
- Cancellation eligibility
- Shipping charges
- Discount eligibility
- Coupon eligibility
- Payment reversal times
- Product specifications
- Product availability
- Stock quantities

If the required information is not present in the available knowledge or live platform data, the issue must be explained as unresolved or escalated.

---

## 2.3 General Policy vs Customer-Specific Information

The following distinction must always be maintained.

### General policy

Example:

> "The platform allows eligible products to be returned according to the applicable return policy."

This can be answered from this document.

### Customer-specific question

Example:

> "Is order ORD-123 eligible for a return?"

This requires actual order information.

The Support Agent must not determine customer-specific eligibility from the policy document alone.

# 4. Product Troubleshooting

## 4.1 General Troubleshooting Principle

Before troubleshooting a product, the Support Agent should identify:

1. Product type
2. Problem being experienced
3. Whether the product is powered on
4. Whether the issue is reproducible
5. Whether the issue appears to be physical damage
6. Whether the product may require warranty or technical support

The Support Agent should not provide unsafe or destructive instructions.

# 5. Laptop Troubleshooting

## 5.1 Laptop Does Not Turn On

If a laptop does not turn on, the customer should:

1. Confirm that the charger is properly connected.
2. Confirm that the power adapter is connected to a working electrical outlet.
3. Leave the laptop connected to power for several minutes.
4. Attempt to power the laptop on again.
5. If the laptop still does not turn on, contact technical support.

The customer should not disassemble the laptop unless instructed by an authorized service provider.

---

## 5.2 Laptop Is Not Charging

If a laptop is not charging:

1. Check whether the charger is securely connected.
2. Check the charging indicator if available.
3. Try a known working electrical outlet.
4. Check whether the charging cable shows visible damage.
5. If the issue continues, contact technical support.

Customers should not attempt electrical repairs themselves.

---

## 5.3 Laptop Overheating

For overheating concerns:

1. Place the laptop on a hard, flat surface.
2. Ensure that ventilation openings are not blocked.
3. Avoid using the laptop on soft surfaces such as beds or cushions.
4. Close unnecessary applications.
5. Allow the laptop to cool down if it becomes unusually hot.
6. If overheating continues, contact technical support.

The Support Agent must not recommend opening the laptop or modifying internal components.

---

# 6. Smartphone Troubleshooting

## 6.1 Smartphone Does Not Turn On

Customers should:

1. Connect the phone to its charger.
2. Wait for the charging indicator.
3. Check the charging cable and adapter for visible damage.
4. Try powering the device on again.
5. If the device remains unresponsive, contact technical support.

---

## 6.2 Smartphone Battery Drains Quickly

Customers may:

1. Check which applications are using the most battery.
2. Close unnecessary applications.
3. Reduce screen brightness.
4. Disable unnecessary background services.
5. Install available official software updates.

If abnormal battery drain continues, technical support should be contacted.

# 7. Damaged Product Policy

## 7.1 Product Arrived Damaged

If a customer reports that a product arrived physically damaged:

1. The customer should stop using the product if continued use may cause additional damage or safety concerns.
2. The customer should preserve the product packaging where possible.
3. The customer should keep the product, accessories, and included components.
4. The customer may be asked to provide photographs or other evidence of the damage.
5. The issue should be evaluated under the platform's applicable damaged-product procedure.

The Support Agent must not automatically promise:

- A replacement
- A full refund
- A partial refund
- A specific compensation amount

unless the relevant policy or live system confirms the outcome.

---

## 7.2 Safety-Related Damage

If a product appears electrically unsafe, emits smoke, sparks, unusual heat, or presents another immediate safety concern:

1. Stop using the product.
2. Disconnect it from power if it is safe to do so.
3. Do not attempt to repair it.
4. Contact the platform's support team.

Safety-related cases should be escalated for appropriate handling.

---

# 8. Wrong Product Policy

## 8.1 Customer Received the Wrong Product

If a customer receives a product different from what was ordered:

1. The customer should verify the order details.
2. The customer should not use the incorrect product if use could affect the return or investigation.
3. The customer should retain the packaging and included accessories.
4. The issue should be reported to support.
5. The platform may request photographs or other information for verification.

The Support Agent must not promise a specific resolution without checking the applicable process and customer order information.

---

# 9. Missing Item Policy

## 9.1 Missing Item From an Order

If an order contains multiple products and one or more expected items are missing:

1. The customer should compare the delivered package with the order details.
2. The customer should check whether the order was split into multiple shipments.
3. The customer should check the shipment information if available.
4. If the item is not part of another shipment, the customer should contact support.

The Support Agent must not assume that an item is missing simply because it was not included in the first package.

# 15. Payment Support

## 15.1 Payment Failed

If payment fails:

1. Verify whether the order was actually created.
2. Verify the payment status.
3. Do not assume that a failed payment means the order was placed.
4. If money was deducted but the order was not successfully created, the issue should be investigated through the payment/order system.

---

## 15.2 Money Deducted but Order Not Created

The Support Agent should distinguish between:

- Payment authorization
- Successful payment
- Order creation
- Payment reversal/refund

A payment transaction does not automatically mean an order was successfully placed.

The Support Agent should check the actual order and payment records.

---

## 15.3 Duplicate Payment

If the customer believes they were charged twice:

1. Verify the relevant payment records.
2. Determine whether both transactions succeeded.
3. Check whether both transactions are associated with orders.
4. Escalate if the payment status cannot be resolved automatically.

The Support Agent must not immediately promise a refund without verification.

# 20. Escalation Policy

## 20.1 When to Escalate

The issue should be escalated when:

1. Required policy information is unavailable.
2. Retrieved knowledge is insufficient.
3. Customer-specific information cannot be verified.
4. The system cannot determine the customer's eligibility.
5. The customer reports a safety-related issue.
6. A payment issue cannot be resolved using available system data.
7. A refund status is inconsistent or unavailable.
8. A shipment status cannot be determined.
9. The issue requires manual review.
10. The customer disputes a system decision that cannot be automatically resolved.
11. The customer requests an exception to an existing policy.
12. The requested resolution is not defined in the available policy.

---

## 20.2 Unsupported Policy Questions

If a customer asks:

> "Will you compensate me ₹20,000 for this inconvenience?"

and no compensation policy exists in the knowledge base, the Support Agent must not invent one.

The response should explain that the available support information does not define such compensation and that the case requires further review.

---

## 20.3 Manual Review

Manual review may be necessary when:

- Evidence needs to be evaluated.
- A customer disputes the recorded order state.
- A refund cannot be automatically verified.
- A product damage claim requires investigation.
- A policy exception is requested.
- Multiple systems contain inconsistent information.
